What private browsing does

Private browsing (Incognito in Chrome, Private in Safari/Firefox) stops your browser from saving history, cookies, and form entries on your device after you close the window. It does not make you invisible online — websites, your employer, school, or internet provider can still see your activity.

What to do

  • Use private browsing on shared or public devices. Library computers, a friend's tablet, or any device you don't own: open a private window so you don't leave passwords or history behind.
  • Use it for one-off searches. Shopping for a gift, looking up a medical topic, or checking prices without cookies affecting results — private windows keep those searches off your device history.
  • Close the window when done. Private browsing only protects you while the window is open.
  • Remember the limits. Private browsing doesn't hide your IP address, stop tracking by websites, or protect you from malware.

Private browsing checklist

  • Know how to open a private window in your browser (Ctrl+Shift+N / Cmd+Shift+N in most browsers)
  • Use private windows on shared or public devices
  • Close private windows when finished
  • Don't rely on private browsing to hide your activity from websites or your network
  • Clear downloads separately — private browsing may keep downloaded files

5. Phone & email hygiene

← Back to home

Phone hygiene

Your phone number is tied to many accounts. Protecting it reduces spam and SIM-swap risk.

  • Don't post your number publicly. Social media, resumes, and public profiles are scraped by bots.
  • Use a secondary number for sign-ups. Free apps like Google Voice give you a number for shopping, newsletters, and accounts — keeping your real number off marketing lists.
  • Don't answer unknown calls. Let them go to voicemail. Scammers spoof real-looking numbers.
  • Set a PIN on your phone carrier account. Call your carrier and ask to add a port/PIN lock.

Email hygiene

Your inbox is the gateway to most of your accounts.

  • Use separate emails for different purposes. One for personal/family, one for shopping/newsletters, one for banking.
  • Don't click links in unexpected emails. Hover to see where a link goes. When in doubt, visit the company's website directly.
  • Unsubscribe from mailing lists you don't read. Less inbox clutter means fewer phishing emails can hide.
  • Turn on spam filtering. Most providers do this by default; check that it's on.

Phone/email checklist

  • Don't post your personal phone number on public profiles or social media
  • Consider a secondary number (e.g., Google Voice) for sign-ups and shopping
  • Set a port/PIN lock with your phone carrier
  • Use a separate email for shopping/newsletters vs. personal/banking
  • Don't click links in unexpected emails; visit sites directly instead
  • Unsubscribe from mailing lists you no longer read

2. Two-factor authentication

← Back to home

What 2FA is

Two-factor authentication adds a second step after your password. Even if someone has your password, they still can't get in without the second factor — usually a code from your phone or an app.

What to do

  • Turn on 2FA where it matters most. Start with your email, your phone carrier, and your bank. Email is the most important because attackers use it to reset passwords everywhere else.
  • Pick the right method. An authenticator app (Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator, Authy) is better than text messages (SMS). SMS can be intercepted in rare cases. Use SMS if it's your only option — it's still much better than nothing.
  • Save backup codes. When you turn on 2FA, the site gives you backup codes. Print them or save them somewhere safe. If you lose your phone, backup codes let you back in.

2FA checklist

  • Turn on 2FA for your email account
  • Turn on 2FA for your phone carrier account
  • Turn on 2FA for your bank and other money accounts
  • Prefer an authenticator app over text messages when available
  • Save backup codes somewhere safe (printed, at home)
  • Test that 2FA works (log out and log back in once)

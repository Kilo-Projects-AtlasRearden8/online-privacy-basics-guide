Why passwords matter

A password is the key to your account. If someone guesses it or steals it from a leak, they can get in. Two things keep passwords safe:

  • Length — longer is stronger
  • Uniqueness — never reuse the same password on more than one site

What to do

  • Use a password manager. It creates and stores strong passwords for you, so you only need to remember one main password. Built-in managers (Apple Passwords, Google Password Manager) work well for most people.
  • Make your main password long and memorable. Think of a sentence or a few unrelated words strung together. Long and silly is better than short and weird.
  • Never reuse passwords. If one site leaks, attackers try that same password everywhere. A unique password per site stops that.
  • Don't write passwords on paper or email them to yourself. If you must write one down, keep the paper safe at home — never in your wallet.
  • Don't share your password. Real support teams will never ask for your password.

Password checklist

  • Install or turn on a password manager (Apple Passwords, Google Password Manager, Bitwarden)
  • Change your most important accounts first (email, bank, phone carrier)
  • Give each account its own unique password
  • Make your manager's main password long and memorable
  • Save your manager's recovery code somewhere safe (printed, at home)
  • Never reuse a password on two different sites
